 # Ending: The Memory Beneath the Flame

The wind tugs at your wings as you rise from the valley, higher than before—not in challenge, but in farewell. Below, the knight watches, his sword sheathed, eyes not with fear, but wonder. The villagers don’t cheer. They simply watch, silent, sensing that something ancient stirs within you—something older than wrath, older than fire.

The sky opens above you, and you follow the pull in your chest. It's not instinct. It's something deeper—like a half-remembered song, calling you home.

Mountains fall away beneath you. Rivers braid across the land like veins of silver. Beyond the frost-touched cliffs lies the place you swore had only existed in dreams: Ashmere Hollow. The cradle of dragonkind, lost in time, buried by the world’s forgetting.

You land upon stone worn smooth by talons long turned to dust. Pillars rise, broken and overgrown, not by decay—but by peace. And at the center: a circle of obsidian mirrors, cracked but humming with memory.

As you approach, the mirrors shimmer—not with your reflection, but with selves.
A child, human, laughing by firelight.
A young squire, trembling as he touched a dragon's scale.
A wyrmling, roaring in pain beneath the stars.
A scholar pouring over runes in the dark.
A dragon crowned in grief.
A lover mourning flame-lit loss.
Each face is yours. Each story, a shard.

The truth dawns: You were not born Flameborn. You were made.
Not by sorcery or curse, but by choice repeated through lifetimes. Reincarnation—draconic and mortal—woven by an ancient pact made in the world’s first language: the language of flame and heart.

A voice, neither wholly yours nor wholly other, speaks within the stone:

“To remember all that you were is to carry all that you can be. Flameborn is not a title, but a question: What will you burn away, and what will you become?”

The mirrors crack fully now, releasing not destruction—but clarity. The fire within you steadies, no longer ravenous. You see with both dragon eyes and mortal heart.

You could return to the village. Guard them. Lead them.
But instead, you sit in the circle, wings folded like prayer.
You write your story into the stone—not to be worshipped, but to be remembered.

And one day, when the world forgets again, another will find this place.
And they will see your reflection—dragon and human, flame and memory.
And they will choose.

You are Flameborn. And now, you understand why.
